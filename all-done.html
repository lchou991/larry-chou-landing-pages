Surrounding Markets

What the comps say.

95118 prices below neighboring 95124 and 95125. Running comps puts this home at $1.78M–$1.82M. That number is real, but it’s a floor, not a ceiling, if you read the micro correctly.

Local Market

What the comps miss.

This specific pocket of 95118 feeds into Branham High School, not Pioneer. That distinction matters to a specific buyer who will pay for it. Comps don’t capture it because most buyers hunting for Branham aren’t searching in this zip code. They don’t know it qualifies.

Buyer Pool

Who pays the most for this home.

The buyers who will pay the highest price for this home are school district buyers priced out of 95124 and 95125. These are buyers who would stretch their budget to get into the right school boundary.

If schools aren’t a priority, there are better homes in 95118 at lower prices. Not dramatically cheaper, but cheaper. You get more home for what this home sold for. Those buyers won’t pay the most. They’re not our target.

The buyers who will pay the most care about one thing: Branham over Pioneer. This was confirmed by commentary from open house visitors.

That insight drove the entire prep strategy. We didn’t need to be the best-presented home in 95118. We needed to be the best-presented home at this price point, for this square footage, this layout, this livability, when stacked against Leigh and other desirable nearby school boundaries. We would be the step down from those premium pockets for buyers who can’t quite stretch to 95124 or 95125 without compromises in the home they want. And the step up from lower 95118 pricing where Pioneer is the boundary.

The Home

What we were working with.

Needs-work condition: dated finishes, cluttered presentation, dingy floors. Underneath: 28-year-old roof, 35-year-old HVAC, active termites, active plumbing leak in the master bath.

We repositioned the home as move-in ready, without touching the deferred maintenance underneath.

Prep

What we did and didn’t fix.

We did not fix the roof, the HVAC, the termites, or the plumbing leak.

This pocket of 95118 has a defined price ceiling. $40,000 in repairs does not raise it.

Spending that money carried a high risk of capping at the same price. It would have handed the buyer a $40,000 upgrade directly out of our seller’s pocket. We kept that money in the seller’s wallet and ran only the prep that moved the needle.

Selected Positioning (Pricing)

How we priced it.

Option 1: $1.488M

Drives wrong-profile buyers. Helps the agent advertise they sold $400K over asking. Risks failing and forcing a price increase. Only valid in specific situations. Not here.

Option 2: $1.70M–$1.79M

Sends the wrong signal. Gets lumped in with lower 95118 inventory. $1.79M is only $10K below $1.8M but it is a materially weaker signal. Higher probability of capping in the low $1.8Ms.

Option 3: $1.85M+

Now competing with 95124 near Campbell, Leigh High boundaries, and Willow Glen. This pocket of 95118 loses pricing power in that matchup.

$1.8M exactly.

Separated the home from lower priced 95118 inventory. Signaled value to more desirable adjacent area school district buyers.

Why I created 16DayPrep™.

I represented a buyer whose seller had spent over $50,000 prepping the home before listing. We replaced the cabinets they’d repainted, the windows where they’d painted the trim, and ripped out the floors they’d refinished.

About $47,000 of their prep was waste.

I realized that when it’s time to actually sell, most homeowners get pushed into one of four broken paths:

Sell As-Is

Leave money on the table.

Do It Yourself

Months managing contractors and burning weekends.

Fake Concierge

Hidden fees. Inflated invoices. Incentives work against you.

Commodity Realtor

Volume churners, discounters, or part-time friends. Anyone can list, but your net outcome suffers.

A Better Choice

I told my client upfront that if I took total ownership of the prep and sale, he had to let me use the property as a case study and show other homeowners the difference in how a sale should be executed. He agreed.

I took total ownership of the sale at 1458 Portobelo so my client only had to approve key decisions and pay invoices remotely. I developed the sale strategy, the prep checklist, the estimated costs, and the timeline. He approved, and 16DayPrep™ was born.

Why Exactly 16 Days?

For a Bay Area home under 2,500 sq ft, 16 days is the sweet spot with a small built-in buffer. It’s just enough time to fix the things that kill buyer perception without taking on unnecessary risk.

It also leaves enough time for cleaning, inspections, staging, photos/videos, and pre-launch marketing.

Larger and more costly prep work starts to increase execution risk and hits diminishing returns. As a homeowner you don’t want to step into the role of a flipper. The Bay Area has some of the worst margins for flippers in the nation. Many only target a 7% profit. The risk-reward just isn’t worth it.

For larger homes, 16DayPrep™ scales predictably: 19 days for 2,500+ sq ft, and 22 days above 3,000 sq ft.
